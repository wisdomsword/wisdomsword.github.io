From Dujiangyan to Agent Harnesses:
Six Engineering Design Principles for Long-Lived AI Systems

What a 2,200-year-old hydraulic system teaches us about selective offloading, hard capacity boundaries, verifiable baselines, and maintenance as a runtime function in LLM agent harnesses.

Abstract

Motivation

An agent harness — the layer of routing, memory, tool dispatch, sandboxing, and guardrails that wraps a foundation model — faces a load-management problem structurally similar to the one the Dujiangyan irrigation system has solved on the Min River for more than twenty-two centuries: an input stream that is externally determined, heavy-tailed, and heterogeneous, carrying useful signal together with material that degrades the system if retained. Neither system controls its input; both must decide what to admit, what to divert, and what to flush.

Method

We treat Dujiangyan as an evidence-graded engineering case study, using the hydraulic system as a source of testable analogies rather than as a software metaphor. We assemble the engineering record (geometry, hydrology, prototype sediment observations, and the historical maintenance regime), recompute the governing relations, and record each quantity with two separate labels — one for source grade (A: peer-reviewed or archival original; B: official secondary or indexed; C: tertiary) and once for claim strength (whether page numbers, table numbers, same-section pairing, and independent replication are available). We then extract design principles that survive translation into software, discarding those that depend on hydraulic specifics.

Findings

Three engineering facts carry over: selective offloading, hard capacity boundaries, and institutionalized maintenance. We operationalize them as six normative principles, C1–C6 (selective offloading; hard capacity boundaries; verifiable baselines; field signals vs. explicit authorization; maintenance institutionalization; compositional structure and failure isolation), each with a six-field acceptance contract. The reproducible observation anchoring the argument is that the outer channel diverts 35.8–39.1% of the water while carrying away 42.3–52.8% of suspended sediment and roughly 74% of gravel bedload. Absent paired annual data, the conservative endpoints for the suspended-sediment selectivity ratio are κ ∈ [1.08, 1.47]; other κ values derive from different years or stated assumptions and must not be pooled into a single "measured interval."

Contributions

(i) A two-axis evidence-grading scheme that separates provenance from inferential strength, so that a well-sourced number is not silently promoted into a well-tested claim. (ii) Six principles stated as auditable contracts rather than slogans. (iii) An explicit counterexample and cost register (Section 8), including the cases where Dujiangyan fails to be a model — most importantly, where central control was added, with a documented trade-off in adaptive capacity.

Scope and non-claims

This is not a hydraulics paper, and we make no claim that fluid mechanics governs software systems. The analogy is used generatively — to propose candidate invariants — and every transferred claim is restated as a project-level hypothesis with a measurement protocol. Where the historical record is incomplete, we say so and mark the item as awaiting primary-source confirmation rather than smoothing it over.

1.1  A One-Page Summary for the Non-Specialist Reader Overview  C1 · C2 · C3 · C4 · C5 · C6

This section summarizes the engineering facts, the boundary of the analogy, and the six principles; the evidence and acceptance contracts follow in Sections 2–7.

What is this thing?
Dujiangyan is a diversion, sediment-exclusion, and irrigation system on the Min River in Sichuan, begun around 256 BCE. It still delivers irrigation and municipal water, but its operation now also depends on gated structures, reservoir dispatch, and annual maintenance.
What problem does it solve?
The Min River delivers water, sand, and gravel simultaneously. Dujiangyan combines flow division, sediment exclusion, and periodic dredging so that comparatively little sediment reaches the irrigation district. Its longevity cannot be attributed to any single component.
How does it actually work?
Three mechanisms act together — topography, gated/reservoir dispatch, and annual maintenance:
  ① A levee called the Fish Mouth splits the river at the bend; because the flow turns, the centrifugal effect sends cleaner surface water into the inner channel and sediment-laden bed water into the outer channel.
  ② A 20-meter-wide throat cut through rock, Baopingkou, constrains how much water can enter under a given head.
  ③ A low side spillway, Feishayan (crest height about 2 m above the bed), can spill once the reported hydraulic threshold is reached, carrying sediment with it.
One number you can check
In 1970–1973 records, the outer channel took roughly 36–39% of the water and 42–53% of the suspended sediment. A separate 1975–1978 record reports about 74% of gravel entering the outer channel; the two datasets are not same-year pairs. A further secondary source reports that under 8% of total sediment reaches the Baopingkou throat; the original experimental page number remains to be confirmed.
What does this have to do with AI systems?
The "harness" we now build around large models is under analogous pressure: stale context, repeated errors, failed retries, and technical debt all accumulate. What transfers is the method — define what must be offloaded, measure whether offloading actually works, and place capacity and authority limits on the execution surface rather than in prose.
The six principles
C1 measures selective offloading; C2 defends capacity boundaries; C3 establishes a recomputable baseline; C4 lets continuous signals rank but never authorize; C5 assigns every maintenance task an owner and a deadline; C6 verifies replacement and isolation through shutdown drills. Each principle requires the project to fill in acceptance thresholds and evidence.

The defining feature of the input is its extreme dynamic range: from 82.7 m³/s observed minimum to 6,400 m³/s observed maximum, a peak-to-trough ratio of 77.39×; including the 1,000-year design flood of 10,120 m³/s gives a design envelope of 122.37×. For any harness facing long-tailed load, the practical instruction is to characterize tail quantiles rather than means. The simulation in [1] accordingly samples three regimes: 800, 1,800, and 10,120 m³/s.

The 6,400 m³/s maximum can be dated to 1964; the cross-section table number is still outstanding. Both Li Yingfa (2013, Table 2) in the Journal of Xihua University ([16]) and the Baidu Baike entry (citing a 1937–1979 series) record that "in 1964 the upper Min River flooded with a peak discharge of 6,400 m³/s, severely damaging the headworks." The three co-existing figures in the literature — 5,480, 5,840, and 6,400 m³/s — are in fact results for the same 1964-07-22 flood measured at three cross-sections: the hydrologic station above Zipingpu, the Zipingpu dam site, and the Dujiangyan headworks. The cross-section difference would make the headworks value 16.8% higher than the upstream value; we record this as an explanation to be verified, not as a resolved discrepancy. The 1933 figure of 10,200 m³/s resulted from the outburst of a landslide-dammed lake formed by the Diexi earthquake and is not part of the natural flood series.

7. Operationalizing the Field Maps to C1, C2, C3, C4, C5, C6

The conclusion of C4 is that a field can reduce the number of repeated rules, but it cannot eliminate rules, false negatives, or conflicts. By "field" here we do not mean a context-window trick for one LLM. We mean the full structural environment a harness needs in order to run inside a real engineering project — taking a lightweight LLM agent such as ForgeAgent as the reference instance: a tool registry and sandbox tiers, a golden-set validation baseline, milestone governance and CI gates, and bounded memory and context. The field supplies a continuous signal for risk ranking; authorization remains a matter of explicit policy and hard boundaries.

7.2  A minimal definition of the field: one continuous scalar ρ per project element

The physical analogy converts into a ranking signal, but software authorization cannot be derived from it automatically. Concretely, in a harness development field: compute a continuous scalar ρ for every element in the repository (module, tool, dependency, task, test) and use it for ordering, sampling, and human review triage; assess blast radius separately.

freshness(x) = exp(−Δ/λ) ρ_priority(x) = α·redundancy + β·(1 − freshness) + γ·failure_rate blast_radius(x) = reachable_assets × privilege_level × failure_impact_scope

Notation. We write blast_radius(x) in full to avoid collision with B, which denotes channel and weir width in Section 5.

Inputs can be harvested from git history, CI, unit tests, and golden-set regression records. Weights, thresholds, and authorization rules still require explicit maintenance:

  • Redundancy — duplication in code and configuration (copy-pasted modules, repeated functions, over-abstraction); more duplication raises the score.
  • Staleness — elapsed time since the element was last modified or referenced, scored as 1 − exp(−Δ/λ) so that older elements receive a larger review signal.
  • Failure rate — the element's failure rate in unit tests and golden regression, including flake rate.
  • Reference count — corroborating evidence for relevance and review priority; it does not offset risk.
  • Blast radius — computed from reachable assets, privilege level, and failure impact scope, and tracked separately from ρ rather than folded into it.

ρ is a continuous signal, not an authorization label. A tool or module may be ordered by ρ and reachability, but routing among trusted, restricted, and untrusted still depends on explicit rules, capability boundaries, and human review. High reference count does not automatically lower risk.

7.4  What "bury the standard underground" means for a field

The buried iron bars have three properties: they sit below the bed surface, they are visible only when excavation reaches them (so they cannot be claimed verbally), and they are the gauge that decides whether the dredge is accepted. In a development field these become:

  • Critical standards need a recomputable baseline. A system's "correct state" cannot rest on an assertion document alone. Whether to adopt event-stream replay depends on consistency and audit requirements.
  • Verification means recomputation, not comparison against a claim. Caches and snapshots accelerate; they are not the truth. Reconcile periodically: reproject from the event stream, compare against current state, and treat any divergence as drift.
  • The gauge must be repeatedly measurable. Candidate measures include golden pass rate, hash or Merkle root, a monotonic sequence number, and latency, token count, and failure rate; the combination is set by the project's audit requirements.
Implementation move

If a project adopts an event-stream approach, it can attach a state_hash to critical state, maintain a golden set, and provide replay(from_seq). These are project-specific choices, not prescriptions that follow necessarily from Dujiangyan.

7.5  Hard-boundary checks must run continuously

The physical facts about the Feishayan are that 728.25 m is a geometric constant, that water cannot negotiate with it, and that the traditional maxim explicitly forbids raising the crest ("keep the weir low"). Translated into a development field:

  • Put thresholds where they cannot be changed at runtime. Kernel constant files, read-only configuration, or hash-locked configuration sections; CI gates require review and an event trail for any change. Thresholds must not live in prompts, documents, or agent-writable configuration; crossing a trust boundary still requires a hard gate.
  • Executing a check and refusing on a check are two different things. Applicable ruff, pytest, golden, and sandbox policies should run on every commit or task. Zero refusals may simply mean healthy input; on its own it says nothing about whether the threshold is set correctly.
  • Track gate health as separate statistics. At minimum, record execution coverage, true-defect detection rate, false-positive rate, and bypass count. Whether to set a target band is a project risk decision.
  • Raising a threshold transfers cost into the future. Raising the weir diverts more water in the dry season (the KPI improves) but reduces flood-season sediment export, raising the bed and progressively disabling the system. The software equivalents are relaxing a circuit breaker, skipping schema validation, or letting untrusted code execute directly: short-term pass rates rise, and the system silts up.
Verifiable criterion

For applicable changes, CI check execution coverage must meet the project's registered value. No universal nonzero target is set for rejection rate. Privilege bypasses must be zero. False positives and false negatives are reviewed periodically against labeled samples. κ applies only to bypass channels with a defined harmful load.

7.6  Minimum viable implementation (four steps)

  1. Compute ρ for every project element (module, tool, dependency, task), record blast radius separately, and log weights, thresholds, and data provenance.
  2. Establish three main channels — trusted, restricted, untrusted. Routing is an explicit policy f(ρ, blast radius), deny-by-default.
  3. Make the settling layer an untrusted isolation sandbox, purged in batches per milestone ("dredge the shoal deep"); failures do not flow back into the core.
  4. Write offload thresholds and chokepoint ceilings (tokens, memory, sandbox tier) into kernel constants, protected by CI gates ("keep the weir low").

10. Discussion and Conclusion Recaps C1, C2, C3, C4, C5, C6

Dujiangyan shows that long-lived operation rests jointly on selective offloading, capacity boundaries, verifiable baselines, and a maintenance regime. Mapped onto a harness: C1 governs offload quality, C2 constrains capacity, C3 supplies the acceptance baseline, C4 separates ranking from authorization, C5 assigns maintenance accountability, and C6 verifies replacement and isolation. Specific thresholds and implementations are determined by project data.

We want to be explicit about what we are not claiming. The hydraulic system is not evidence about software, and the strongest numbers in this report — the κ endpoints of 1.08 and 1.47 — are conservative endpoints computed across unpaired years, not a fitted effect size. The Manning computation in Section 5.3 recovers a design value by construction. The <8% figure at Baopingkou rests on a relay of an experiment whose page number we have not located. We have kept these weaknesses visible rather than laundering them into firm-sounding conclusions, because the discipline being transferred here — that a standard must be recomputable, measured, and attributable — applies to our own argument first.

What does survive, and what we would defend to any team building an agent harness today, is the shape of the design rather than any number in it: define the debris before you build the filter; put the ceiling in the kernel, not the prompt; let continuous signals rank but never authorize; make maintenance an object with an owner and a due date; and pay for every central control point by writing down what you gave up. These are cheap to state and expensive to retrofit, which is arguably the most transferable property Dujiangyan has.

Appendix. Heuristics 1–12: Evidence and Method

The twelve heuristics preserve only engineering facts, mechanisms, equations, and applicability boundaries. Project actions and acceptance criteria are governed by C1–C6.

Expand the twelve method cards
H1

Sediment ratio > flow ratio

Governed by C1 Selective offloading · Dujiangyan original: sediment ratio exceeds flow ratio
Engineering fact

The 1970–1973 records give outer-channel flow of 35.8–39.1% and suspended-sediment diversion of 42.3–52.8%. The figure of about 74% of gravel bedload comes from 1975–1978 records and cannot be treated as a same-year pair.
